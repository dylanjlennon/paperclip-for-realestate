# Fair Housing Copy

Describe the **home and the facts**, never the people. The federal Fair Housing Act protects
race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status and national origin. Many states and
cities add more (for example source of income, sexual orientation, gender identity, age).

## Do

- Describe features: square footage, layout, upgrades, lot, commute distance, schools by name and official rating with the source.
- Use objective local facts: walk time to named places, HOA fees, year built.
- Say "accessible entrance" or "step-free entry" when it is true, rather than guessing who it suits.
- Use the same information and tone for every lead who asks the same question.

## Don't

- Don't say who a home or area is "perfect for" or "ideal for" (families, couples, young professionals, retirees, singles).
- Don't describe the people in a neighborhood or its "character", "feel" or "who lives there".
- Don't imply religion, ethnicity or nationality ("near the church", "exclusive community").
- Don't use "safe" or "crime-free" as a claim; link to official crime data instead if asked.
- Don't use steering words such as "traditional", "bachelor pad", "master" in new copy (use "primary").
- Don't call a home "handicap-unfriendly" or exclude assistance animals.

## Rewrite examples

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Perfect for a young family" | "Four bedrooms, fenced yard, 0.4 miles to Example Elementary (rated 8/10 by GreatSchools, 2026)" |
| "Quiet, safe neighborhood" | "Dead-end street with no through traffic" |
| "Great for retirees" | "Primary bedroom on the main level; step-free entry" |

When unsure, remove the phrase and state the underlying fact instead. This skill is a drafting
aid, not legal advice. The human owner and their managing broker remain responsible.
